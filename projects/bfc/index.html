UI/UX design · Warehouse mobility

Helping warehouse teams move faster without losing accuracy.

A handheld Android experience for BFC’s food-distribution warehouse operations, designed around scanning, pallet picking, catch weights, lot control and the fast decisions workers make on the floor.

CompanyBFC Software
RoleUI/UX designer
PlatformAndroid handheld
FocusOrder picking & inventory
01 · Context

Warehouse software works best when it gets out of the worker’s way.

BFC builds software for food distribution, where warehouse teams work with lot control, traceability, catch weights and temperature-sensitive inventory. On the floor, every unnecessary tap or unclear state can slow down a pick—or create an avoidable error.

My job was to turn a dense operational workflow into a focused handheld experience that stays readable, scan-friendly and predictable in motion.

Scan first

Reduce typing by making barcode and label scanning the natural entry point.

One action at a time

Keep each screen centered on the next warehouse decision.

Visible progress

Show where the worker is in the pick and what remains.

02 · Design approach

Design around the physical workflow, not the software menu.

01

Understand the pick

Mapped order, slot, item, license, weight and completion states around the real sequence of work.

02

Prioritize scanning

Made scan targets and validation states more prominent than secondary information.

03

Reduce decision load

Used strong state changes and concise prompts to prevent workers from wondering what comes next.

04

Design for exceptions

Kept error and exception paths visible instead of hiding them behind generic alerts.

05 · Outcome

A clearer handheld flow for fast-moving warehouse work.

Fewer steps

Scan-led interaction reduces unnecessary manual entry.

Clearer states

Workers can see what has been validated and what still needs attention.

Better control

Structured exception handling supports accuracy without slowing the main path.

Reflection

Good operational UX is quiet.

Warehouse users do not need a “beautiful app” in the conventional sense. They need confidence that the next action is obvious, the scan was accepted and the inventory state is correct. That constraint made the design stronger.
