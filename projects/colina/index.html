01 — Context & problem

Insurance service was fragmented across office visits, manual requests and disconnected information.

Colina offers General, Life and Health insurance solutions. The project brief called for a unified portal where policyholders could access policy information, make payments, manage policies, initiate claims and retrieve documents without repeatedly visiting an insurance office.

The experience had to reduce turnaround time for customers while lowering manual effort for agents and employees.

01

No single view

Customers lacked one place to see policies across multiple lines of business.

02

Office dependency

Payments, updates, documents and claim information relied heavily on offline service.

03

Slow servicing

Manual processes consumed time and resources for both policyholders and staff.

04

Digital expectation

The business needed a modern IT-enabled experience to compete and grow.

02 — Discovery

Research focused on the everyday servicing needs of policyholders.

Discussions with users and heads of different insurance lines highlighted recurring needs: online access to policy information, faster servicing, easier claims, consolidated documents, online payments and a move away from manual processes.

03 — Define

Design principles translated research into a practical self-service model.

01

Unified first

Start with a portfolio-level view before asking users to navigate policy-specific details.

02

Progressive detail

Surface essentials first, then reveal coverage, values, benefits, documents and assignee information.

03

Action near context

Place payment, claim and service actions beside the policy information that triggers them.

04

Explain the process

For claims, combine instructions, forms, upload status and submission in one guided flow.

05 — Key flows

The interface organizes complex insurance tasks into predictable steps.

POLICY

Overview → product → policy → detail

Users move from the cross-policy dashboard into Life or Health lists, then into coverage, values, documents and related actions.

HEALTH

Member → benefits → care → claims

Covered members, digital insurance ID cards, deductible tracking, benefit summaries and care resources remain attached to the health policy.

CLAIM

Prepare → upload → submit → track

The claim flow explains supporting documents and EFT requirements, accepts uploads and then connects to claim history and status.

07 — UX rationale

What makes the portal work.

Portfolio visibility

Users can see Life, Health, Auto, Home and Marine coverage without learning separate systems.

Status clarity

Policy and claim states are visible beside the item, reducing uncertainty and unnecessary support contact.

Document access

Insurance IDs, policy documents, forms and certificates are placed inside the relevant policy context.

Guided servicing

Complex actions such as health claims include instructions before asking the customer to submit.

08 — Intended outcome

Shift routine insurance servicing from branch dependency to digital self-service.

↓ VisitsFewer office-dependent service tasks
↓ TATFaster policy requests and payments
↑ Access24/7 policy and document visibility
↑ ControlClaims and policy servicing in one account

These are intended experience outcomes derived from the supplied project material; the PDF does not provide measured production KPIs.

09 — Reflection

The design challenge was not adding features. It was making five insurance worlds feel like one.

The strongest pattern is consistency: policy categories differ in data and servicing needs, but navigation, hierarchy and actions remain familiar. That lets customers build confidence as they move from simple policy lookup to higher-friction tasks such as claims.
