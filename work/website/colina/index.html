Website design · Insurance

Helping an established insurance brand feel clear, reassuring and current.

Colina has a long history and serves customers across multiple insurance categories. The website needed to balance brand confidence with practical product discovery—making it easier for people to understand what is relevant to them before asking them to take the next step.

01 · About the company

A trusted insurance brand with more than a century of history.

Colina Insurance operates across life, health and general insurance needs. That breadth creates a familiar website problem: customers arrive with a life event or concern in mind, while the organization naturally thinks in product categories.

The design had to bridge those two ways of thinking without losing the authority expected from a financial-services brand.

155+ years of heritage

The visual language needed to feel modern without discarding the sense of permanence and trust.

Multiple insurance lines

Navigation has to support very different customer needs without becoming a product directory.

Trust before action

Product language, proof and service access matter before a quote or contact CTA becomes persuasive.

02 · UX & design approach

Design the site around reassurance and progressive product discovery.

01

Clarify the entry points

Used customer-friendly category labels and clear service pathways so visitors can orient themselves quickly.

02

Build confidence early

Balanced product messaging with brand heritage, proof and support cues rather than leading with sales language alone.

03

Layer information

Kept top-level pages easy to scan while allowing interested visitors to move into deeper product detail.

04

Keep actions contextual

Placed quote, contact and service actions where the customer has enough context to understand why the action matters.

04 · Outcome & impact

A more approachable digital front door for an established insurer.

Clearer

Customers can move from broad needs to relevant insurance information with less cognitive effort.

More human

The content and visual tone support reassurance instead of sounding like a policy document.

More consistent

Shared page patterns help different insurance products feel like one connected brand experience.

Reflection

Trust is not a visual style. It is the result of clarity at every step.

Insurance websites often become dense because the underlying products are complex. The better approach is not to hide that complexity, but to introduce it in the right order and in language customers can act on.
