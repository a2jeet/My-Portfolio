01 · About the company

A global technology partner with a lot to say—and many audiences to say it to.

Damco Solutions is a global technology and digital transformation company working across enterprise applications, cloud, data, AI, product engineering and industry-focused solutions. The site has to speak to decision-makers who arrive with very different levels of technical context.

That made clarity, hierarchy and content architecture as important as the visual layer.

25+ industries represented

The site needs a system flexible enough to handle broad domain expertise.

700+ projects referenced by the company

Proof and capability need to be visible without overwhelming service discovery.

Enterprise buyers

Information must work for both quick scanning and deeper evaluation.

02 · UX & design approach

Start with content architecture, then let the visual system support it.

01

Map the service landscape

Grouped related capabilities and looked for clearer parent-child relationships between services, solutions and industries.

02

Prioritize buyer questions

Structured pages around what the visitor needs to know first: what Damco does, for whom, proof of experience and what to do next.

03

Create repeatable modules

Used modular content patterns so different service pages can stay consistent without becoming identical.

04

Design for handoff

Kept responsive behavior, spacing rules and reusable patterns explicit so the system could scale during development.

04 · Outcome & impact

A website system that can grow with the business.

Clearer IA

Service and industry content can be organized around stronger relationships instead of isolated pages.

Reusable

Modular page patterns support new offerings without redesigning the whole site each time.

Consistent

A shared visual language improves continuity across corporate, service and thought-leadership content.

I’m keeping the impact qualitative here rather than inventing conversion metrics that were not formally measured.

Reflection

Enterprise websites become stronger when the design respects how people read.

The most important work happened before decoration: deciding what belongs together, what deserves emphasis and what can wait. Once the hierarchy is right, visual design can do its real job—help people move through the story with less effort.
